Ingredients
  • 2 large cucumbers
  • 1 celery stalk
  • 1 packed cup kale, tough stems removed
  • 1/4 cup fresh parsley
  • 1/2 lemon, peeled—optional but recommended
Procedure
  1. Wash everything well
  2. Cut into pieces to fit juicer chute
  3. Juice the leafy greens first, alternating them with pieces of cucumber and celery
  4. Finish with cucumber to help push the remaining greens through.
  5. Stir and serve cold.
Notes
This version should contain roughly 75–100 mg of naturally occurring sodium, depending on the produce and how much juice is extracted—considerably less than JuiceLand’s approximately 270 mg per 16 ounces.If you want the absolute minimum, omit the celery entirely and add another half cucumber. It won’t taste exactly like Tigerlilly, but the parsley and lemon will help replace some of celery’s brightness. Don’t add salt.

At approximately 90 mg of sodium, it uses only about 6% of a 1,500-mg daily target or 4% of the FDA’s 2,300-mg Daily Value. For comparison, JuiceLand’s older published nutrition information listed its regular 16-ounce Tigerlilly at 270 mg sodium.

These are estimates because cucumbers vary considerably in size and the Ninja may leave slightly different amounts of nutrients and fiber in the pulp. Juicing removes most—but not all—fiber, while sugars, sodium, and potassium largely remain in the juice. The vegetables’ baseline nutrient values come from USDA/FDA data. FDA raw-vegetable nutrition data

One important note: this juice is high in potassium and vitamin K. That’s ordinarily beneficial, but someone with kidney disease or taking warfarin should check with their clinician and keep vitamin-K intake consistent.
